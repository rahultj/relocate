Implementation Plan · v0.1

A quiet marketplace for
the things you can't take with you.

A single-seller relocation site for offloading household items before a move home. The buyer experience defaults to the path of least resistance — no accounts, no apps, no return visits. SMS over a proxy number carries everything from receipt to chat. One verified phone number per claim is the only friction, and it's the friction that makes everything else trustworthy.

Owner rahultj@gmail.com Target ship 4 weeks to MVP Surface 1 page web + SMS Stack note Twilio · Postgres · Next.js

Three principles, in order

02 · North Star

Every product decision below resolves a tension between buyer ease and seller-side trust. When those collide, the buyer wins; the seller absorbs the complexity. These three rules are how we decide.

P · 01

Buyer-first, always.

If a feature adds a step for the buyer to make the seller's life easier, cut it. The buyer is doing me a favor.

P · 02

SMS is the medium.

Receipts, reminders, chat, cancellations — everything happens in the buyer's existing message thread. No app, no email, no login.

P · 03

OTP is the identity.

One verification at claim time. The verified phone number is the buyer's identity for the lifetime of the listing. No passwords.

✦ A note on the working name

Saudade — a Portuguese word for the longing for what's been left behind. Placeholder; designed to be replaced before launch. The visual system is what should outlive the name.

03 · /[slug]

One scroll. One sort. No accounts.

The landing page is the listing — a single mobile-shaped scroll sorted by the date each item becomes available. The buyer arrives via a link or QR code, sees what's free this week, and taps to claim. There is no homepage, no signup wall, no "browse by city" indirection. The link is the address.

Three pieces of information per row: what it is, when it's free, what it costs. Status is the gating signal — available, waitlist, OTP pending, claimed — and decides whether the price even matters yet.
  • Status as the primary affordance. A buyer can decide whether to bother before reading the description. The colored pulse dot is doing more work than copy could.
  • Price set by the seller. Each item carries a flat amount or is marked Free at listing time. Payment happens in person — cash or UPI — not on platform. AI-suggested pricing from comparable listings is v2.
  • Faded thumbnail + greyed price for claimed items. Don't hide them — claimed-then-freed items rejoin the feed when a holder drops, so visibility primes future returns.
  • Filter chips, not a search bar. The catalog is small. Pre-curated pills cover 95% of intent without keyboard.

Item detail & claim

04 · Conversion moment
04 · /[slug]/[itemId]

The page that asks for the phone number.

This is the only screen where we ask the buyer to do something harder than scroll. Everything before it earns the right to ask; everything after it (the OTP, the SMS receipt, the eventual pickup) is automatic. So we make the ask quiet: one input, one button, and a small visual that proves the waitlist is honest.

The waitlist dots aren't decoration — they're trust. Showing position 3-of-5 tells the buyer their wait is bounded.
  • Phone-number-first. The CTA is a tel-input, not a form. We don't ask for name, email, or pickup time before the OTP.
  • "Free 14 Jun" is the contract. Date prominent, exact, and tied to the listing's stated pickup window. Surprise = trust loss.
  • Waitlist is opt-in even when zero. If the item is claimed, the buyer is offered the waitlist immediately rather than told "come back later."
  • No "1,234 viewers" theater. Passive social proof comes in v2 only when we have honest numbers to show.

05 · /verify

One screen. One job. Then never again.

This is the only screen in the buyer journey that requires more than tap-and-scroll. The friction has to earn its keep — so it's framed as a promise of what it buys you (privacy, no return visits, no spam). The footer is the real CTA: here's why we're asking.

Passwords forward without friction. An OTP can't be screenshotted by a colleague or guessed from a leaked dump. The medium is the verification.
  • Autofill plumbed. iOS will offer the code from the SMS UI; we set the autocomplete="one-time-code" attribute so it lands in one tap.
  • 30-second resend cooldown. Cheaper than rate-limit error states, kinder than nothing.
  • Hashed at rest. The verified phone is stored as a salted hash for lookup; the plaintext is only held in memory during the active session.
  • One number = one identity. Cancel-via-link tokens are scoped to this hash + the specific item, so a leaked link can't be replayed on other items.

06 · proxy thread

One number for everything.

The same proxy number sends the receipt, the reminder, and the forwarded chat. The buyer never has to keep two threads, and never sees the seller's real number — and vice versa. CANCEL is a one-word kill switch that frees the slot and auto-promotes the next person on the waitlist.

The proxy number is the seller. As far as the buyer is concerned, "Rahul's sale" lives at this number — and stops existing 7 days after pickup.
  • Twilio Programmable Messaging + Proxy. The proxy session is scoped per (buyer, item) and times out 7 days post-pickup. After that, replies bounce.
  • Receipt is the source of truth. Includes the cancel link with a short opaque token, scoped to phone+item. Replying CANCEL is the path most people will take; the link is for the rest.
  • 48-hour reminder. Sent at the start of the pickup-day window (configurable per item). Reduces no-shows without becoming spam.
  • The seller dashboard mirrors the thread. Seller sees every conversation in one place on web; buyer sees it in iMessage. Different surfaces, same canonical record.

Seller bulk-add

07 · Seller surface
07 · /seller/add

Photograph the room. Drop the photos. Done.

The seller has 30+ items to list in one sitting. Anything that asks for a form per item is a non-starter. So the flow is: drop a batch of photos, the system pre-fills a draft row per image (suggested name from a vision model, defaulted condition, defaulted available-date inherited from the listing window), and the seller skims and corrects.

Drafts in ochre. The seller's eye is drawn straight to the suggestions that need approval, and skips the rows that look right.
  • Photo-first, fields-second. The thumbnail is the primary key in the seller's head, not the name.
  • Inline editing. No modal-per-item. Tab between rows. Available-date defaults to the listing's start so most rows need zero edits.
  • AI-suggested names are styled italic-ochre to signal "review me." Tapping confirms; typing replaces.
  • Bulk publish. Save the whole batch as one action; QR + link are generated immediately and copied to clipboard.

08 · /seller

A pipeline, not a table.

The seller's mental model is "where is each thing in its journey from listed to gone?" — so the dashboard leans into that. Four pipeline states across the top let the seller see at a glance how much momentum the sale has, and each row is a tap into the proxied SMS thread with that buyer.

Buyer phone numbers are masked even to the seller. They become identifiable only after pickup is confirmed, by which point the trust transfer has happened in person.
  • States: Listed → Interested → Confirmed → Picked up. "Interested" covers both OTP-pending and waitlisted; "Confirmed" is the contracted state where SMS reminders fire.
  • Row click opens the proxied thread. Same thread the buyer sees, mirrored to web with seller-side controls (mark picked up, cancel claim, message).
  • Masked numbers. Only the last three digits show; the proxy address is what the seller actually messages.
  • Listing-level QR / share lives one click away from the header (see next screen).

09 · share modal

One sheet you can tape to the fridge.

Every listing and every individual item gets a short URL and a QR. Both render to a single printable A4 sheet so the seller can put it on the fridge, in the building lobby, or in a WhatsApp message to the apartment group. The link is the invitation.

Short slugs over UUIDs. sdade.in/r/4xq2 is shareable in conversation; a 36-character UUID dies in transmission.
  • Listing QR vs. item QR. The listing QR opens the full feed; the per-item QR opens the detail page directly — useful for tagging individual items in person.
  • Printable A4. A single endpoint returns a clean PDF with the QR, item name, and pickup window — the print job is the marketing.
  • Short, opaque slugs. 4-char base32. Collision-proofed but readable. No PII in the URL.
  • No analytics in MVP. View counts are v2; honest social proof requires a baseline of real traffic first.

Build sequence

12 · Milestones

Four weeks to MVP, in shippable slices. Each milestone is framed by the question it answers — not just what code lands, but what you'd be able to say at the end of that week. Phone is identity from M2 forward; multi-claim architecture is baked in even though its UI ships with v2.

MVP · M1

The listing exists.

Week 1 · ~5 days

  • Question this answers: Does the brand and the visual hold up when a friend sees it?
  • Schema (display subset). listings, items on Postgres. Photo upload to object storage. Claim/buyer tables stub out and ship empty.
  • Public listing page. /r/[slug] renders items sorted by availability date. Filter pills work client-side. Price + Free states both render.
  • Item detail page. Full meta block (price, available-from, condition, pickup). Claim button is visible but disabled with a quiet "Claims open soon" label — so the surface is honest about what's not yet wired.
  • Seller bulk-add (manual). Photo upload + inline form rows. Publish writes records and mints slugs. No AI suggestions yet.
  • QR + printable A4. Listing + per-item QRs generate on publish. You can print and share.
  • What you can do at end of week: Publish your real sale, share the URL with friends, get visual feedback. No buyer-side action possible yet.
MVP · M2

SMS arrives: claim + OTP + receipt.

Week 2 · ~5 days

  • Question this answers: Will real buyers complete the OTP, and does the receipt feel trustworthy in their inbox?
  • Schema additions. claims, buyers, otp_codes, messages tables. claim_session_id nullable on claims from day one — single-claim leaves it null, multi-claim populates it later. No retrofit needed.
  • Twilio outbound. Programmable Messaging plumbed; outbound OTP sends < 2s. MSG91 fallback configured for telco-route redundancy.
  • OTP flow. 6-digit code, 10-min expiry, 5-attempt cap, autocomplete="one-time-code" for iOS autofill.
  • Phone-is-identity-per-listing. Once a buyer verifies, the phone is trusted for the rest of the listing's lifetime — even though the UI ships single-claim. This is the multi-claim hook: the verification reuses architecturally, the multi-tap UI just isn't built yet.
  • Receipt SMS. Templated, includes cancel link with opaque token scoped to (claim_id, phone_hash). 48-hour reminder fires from a cron.
  • What you can do at end of week: A friend can claim one item, get a real OTP, receive a real receipt, and reply CANCEL to drop out.
MVP · M3

Proxy chat, cancel-and-promote, seller view.

Week 3 · ~5 days

  • Question this answers: Can the seller actually run the sale without ever picking up the phone?
  • Twilio Proxy session per (buyer, item). Inbound webhook routes messages with numbers masked both ways. 7-day TTL after pickup, then replies bounce.
  • CANCEL keyword + token URL. Both paths through one handler, both fire waitlist promotion in a single transaction.
  • Waitlist promotion job. Cancel → next entry promoted to pending → SMS sent (skipping OTP if phone already verified for the listing — the M2 hook earns its keep here) → 24h grace window to confirm.
  • Seller dashboard. Pipeline counts (Listed / Interested / Confirmed / Picked up), table view, per-row proxied thread, mark-as-picked-up action.
  • What you can do at end of week: Run a complete sale loop with a friend — claim → coordinate over SMS → pickup → cancel → next person gets promoted automatically.
MVP · M4

Polish, observability, ship.

Week 4 · ~5 days

  • Question this answers: Is this trustworthy enough to send to a stranger?
  • Empty + error states across every surface. Sold-out listing reads honestly. OTP timeout has a path forward. Cancelled-after-pickup is unambiguous.
  • Rate limiting on OTP send (per phone, per IP) and per-thread proxy chat (10 msg/hr). Twilio cost guardrails wired to alerts.
  • Auditable message log. Every inbound/outbound SMS lands in messages with the Twilio SID for reconciliation.
  • Self-serve seller onboarding. Passwordless seller login via SMS OTP — same primitive as the buyer side, no new auth code.
  • Privacy + retention copy. Plain-language page explaining the proxy, the hashing, and the 30-day deletion after listing close.
  • What you can do at end of week: Run your own move-sale start to finish. Real listing, real buyers, real proxy, no manual intervention.
v2

After the first sale.

Post-MVP · prioritized by what hurt most

  • Multi-claim UI. Verified-buyer listing view (one-tap + Claim pills, identity row, "Yours" badges) + bundle receipt SMS with 30s debounce. Schema and verification logic already exist from M2 — this is purely the front-end and SMS-batching layer. See section 13 for the sketch.

13a · listing for a verified buyer

The listing changes shape, quietly.

Once the buyer is verified for this listing (within the pickup window), returning to the page swaps the claim form for a one-tap pill. No form, no OTP, no friction. Items they already hold are checked off and outlined; items they haven't engaged with show the brand-pink + Claim pill. The page recognizes the buyer through a magic-link in the receipt SMS and a cookie that expires when the listing closes.

Verified is a state, not a moment. The buyer earns it once and lives in it for the listing's lifetime.
  • One-tap claim still hits the same handler. The OTP gate is just skipped when the phone is already verified for this listing — no new endpoint, no new code path to maintain.
  • Identity row is a quiet acknowledgement. Forest check + masked number. Not a profile, not a header — just enough that the buyer knows the page knows them.
  • Waitlist is a one-tap pill too. Same affordance, ochre instead of pink, position number inline.
  • Cookie lifetime = listing window + 7 days. Verification dies when the sale dies. No long-lived identity floating around.
13b · bundle receipt

One receipt, many items, one cancel.

When several claims land within ~30 seconds for the same phone, the SMS worker batches them into a single receipt. The buyer gets one tidy summary; the seller dashboard groups them under one entry. Cancel-by-keyword targets the bundle by default; per-item cancels work by typing a shorthand.

Three texts in thirty seconds is spam. One text with three items is a receipt.
  • 30-second debounce in the SMS worker. Claims for the same phone+listing made within the window collapse into one outbound message. Cheap to implement, kind to the inbox.
  • Total line is informational only. No payment is taken on platform. The total just helps the buyer know what to bring.
  • CANCEL alone drops the bundle. CANCEL CHAIR / CANCEL SPEAKER / CANCEL OVEN target individual items. The shorthand is the first noun of the item name; conflict resolution falls back to per-item links from the receipt.
  • Schema gets one nullable column. claim_session_id on claims groups bundle members for receipts and reporting. Single-claim flow leaves it null.

Deliberately out of scope

14 · Saying no

Surfaced here so an engineer reading this can defend "we didn't build that" with the reason, not the reflex.

v2AI-suggested pricing

Sellers set prices in MVP — free or a flat amount per item. The v2 piece is auto-suggesting a price from comparable eBay / FB Marketplace listings; needs scraping infra and adds latency to the listing flow.

v2Passive social proof

Counts before traffic = lies. Defer until we have real numbers worth showing.

v2Estate-sale / pro-seller mode

Different volume, different trust model (deposits, holds). Worth a separate product surface, not a flag.

v2Facebook Marketplace syndication

No Meta API; would be brittle scraping. Revisit when manual cross-posting becomes the daily pain.

v2WhatsApp channel

Meta business approval is 4-6 weeks of admin friction. Start with SMS, add WhatsApp as a non-blocking parallel.

v2Buyer accounts

Defended on first principles. Phone number is identity; accounts add login friction without adding trust.

v2In-app payments

Settle in person, in cash or UPI. Adds KYC/PSP overhead that breaks the "no return visits" promise.

v2Reviews / ratings

Trust comes from who shares the link, not from a 5-star average. A single-seller surface doesn't need a marketplace's reputation layer.

Risks & open questions

15 · Calls to make

Six things that could derail MVP. Each is paired with a mitigation for what I'd ship today, and a flag for the decision that needs to be made by week 2.

Sev · High

SMS vs. WhatsApp as the medium

P · 02 · the medium

In India, WhatsApp is what people open; SMS is what brands send. Buyers may dismiss our messages as promotional. But WhatsApp Business API gates us on a 4-6 week Meta approval before we can ship.

Mitigation for MVP Ship SMS. Send the first message from a Sender ID branded with the seller's first name ("RAHUL"), not a numeric shortcode — same channel, less spam smell. Begin the WhatsApp BSP application in parallel from week 1.
Sev · High

OTP drop-off at the conversion moment

P · 03 · the friction

Even a 6-digit OTP costs 30 seconds. If we lose 40% at this step, the listing dies. Telcos in India have variable deliverability, especially on Jio at peak hours.

Mitigation for MVP Use a transactional SMS provider with telco-routing redundancy (Twilio + MSG91 fallback). Tail OTP send-to-receive latency in dashboards from day 1. Surface "didn't get it?" prominently. If drop-off > 25%, evaluate magic-link as a fallback (loses some trust, preserves conversion).
Sev · High

No-shows & pickup disputes

trust transfer

Buyer claims, never shows up. Seller waits, has to choose between holding the item or freeing the slot. Without payments, we have no economic mechanism to penalize ghosting.

Mitigation for MVP 48-hour reminder + 24-hour grace period after the pickup window opens. If unresponsive in the proxy thread, seller can mark "no-show" from the dashboard, which auto-promotes the next waitlist entry. Cost is borne by the next-in-line, not the seller.
Sev · Med

Proxy session abuse

SMS surface

A buyer with a claim could spam the seller over the proxy thread. Or a buyer's number gets leaked via the dashboard. Or someone tries to reuse a verified phone across listings to bypass per-listing limits.

Mitigation for MVP Per-thread rate limit (10 messages / hour). Seller has a "block this buyer" action that closes the proxy session. Mask buyer numbers in the dashboard until pickup is confirmed.
Sev · Med

The "single seller" assumption

product shape

The plan assumes one listing = one seller leaving. If a building has 4 families relocating in the same month, the experience fragments into 4 disjoint URLs.

Mitigation for MVP Out of scope — this is MVP. But flag for v2: allow co-located listings to be aggregated under a neighborhood-tag URL (/n/indiranagar) that's an opt-in roll-up, not a marketplace.
Sev · Low

Slug collisions at scale
